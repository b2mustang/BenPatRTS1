Footprint: Paper Prototype

Version 0.2 · one side contract, solo · tests the core loop before any code

What you need

  • This page, printed. Cut out the operation cards (they print three per row).
  • A pencil and a few coins or tokens.
  • A 10-sided die, or any dice app. A "0" on a d10 counts as 10.
  • About 30–45 minutes per contract.

Contract: Corrigan Freight

Steal the client ledger from Corrigan Freight's billing server before the end of Day 3.

Payout: $3,000. You lose if Target Alert reaches 12 (lockdown), Heat reaches 10 (raid) or time runs out.

New in 0.2

  • Time is shown as parts of a day. Each turn is one 4-hour part of the day, and the clock shows every day with the same six parts. Card times are in hours.
  • Every card says what the action is in plain language, and shows what happens on success and on failure.
  • Words to know and your first turn explain the keys, the tracks and running operations side by side.
  • Gear matters more: Spear-Phish needs a Lookalike domain, and two new items improve phone calls and in-person work.
  • Lying low works better: Target Alert drops by 2, not 1, in a turn with no Noise.
  • The rulings from the first playtest are now part of the rules.

Words to know

Foothold
Your first machine or account inside Corrigan's network. Almost everything else needs it.
Admin credentials
An administrator's login, which opens most doors inside the network.
Route
A path into the billing network, which is walled off from the normal office network.
MFA bypass
A way past the second login step: the phone prompt or code that staff use after their password.
Intel
What you've learned about the target. Spend it to improve a roll.
Noise → Target Alert
Noise is anything their security team might notice. It raises Target Alert: how suspicious Corrigan is right now.
Trail
Evidence that leads back to you. When Corrigan investigates, Trail turns into Heat and helps them find your Foothold.
Heat
How close the police are to you personally. It never goes down during a contract.
Laptop and Body slots
Your hands. Two Laptop slots run digital and remote work; the Body slot is you, physically somewhere. All three can run at the same time.

Setup

  • The clock starts at Day 1, Morning (turn 1). You are at your Desk.
  • Cash $1,000. Intel 0, Heat 0, Target Alert 0, Trail 0.
  • Slots: 2 Laptop slots and 1 Body slot.
  • Known nodes: the Website and Staff email. Everything else on the network map is hidden until revealed.

Time: parts of the day

Each turn is one part of the day: 4 in-game hours. Every day has the same six parts. When you do something matters, because offices, phones and guards follow the clock.

How a turn works

Each turn, do these steps in order:

  1. Shop and move.
    • Buy gear from the shop. It's yours immediately.
    • Travel between your Desk and the Café or HQ: put "Travel" in your Body slot. It takes this whole turn, and you arrive at the end of it.
    • Walk between the Café and HQ: they're next door, so this is free and doesn't use your Body slot.
  2. Start operations. Place cards in free slots and pay their cost.
    • Laptop operations need a Laptop slot, and you must be at your Desk or the Café, either before or after your walk this turn.
    • Body operations need your Body slot, and you must be at the place shown after moving. While a Body operation runs, you stay where it is.
    • Check the card's Needs, including ☀ business hours or ☾ night. Needs are checked only when you start. A running Laptop operation keeps going wherever you go.
    • You may cancel a running operation: no refund, no Noise, and its slot frees up.
  3. Run. Every running operation counts down 4 hours, including ones you started this turn: a 4-hour operation resolves at the end of the turn you start it. Resolve finished operations one at a time, in the order you choose:
    • Spend Intel if you want: +10% per point, up to +30%. Decide before you roll. Spent Intel is gone.
    • Apply any Alert penalty from the table below, using Target Alert as it was at the start of this step.
    • Roll the d10. Success if the roll is at or under the chance ÷ 10 (40% succeeds on 1–4).
    • Apply the card's ✓ or ✗ line: its effect, its Noise (added to Target Alert), its Trail and any Heat.
    • Keys don't stack: winning a key you already hold gives nothing, but its Noise and Trail still count.
    • Recon pays once: a recon card gives its Intel and reveal only the first time it succeeds.
  4. Defenders. Apply the Target Alert table below. Then, if no Noise was added this turn, lower Target Alert by 2 (not below 0).
  5. Clock. Mark the next part of the day. After Day 3, Late (turn 16), the contract fails.

Your first turn: three things at once

You have three "hands": two Laptop slots and your Body slot. They all work at the same time, so a strong turn keeps all three busy.

For example, on Day 1 Morning at your Desk:

  • Laptop 1: OSINT Sweep, reading up on the company's staff (4 hours).
  • Laptop 2: Perimeter Scan, mapping their internet-facing systems (4 hours).
  • Body: Travel to the Café, closer to HQ (takes the turn).

At the end of the turn both scans resolve and you've arrived at the Café, ready for business-hours work next to HQ. Longer operations keep running in their slot while you start others around them.

The four keys

Exfiltrating the ledger needs all four: a Foothold, Admin credentials, a Route and an MFA bypass. Each key can be earned more than one way: digital, physical or social.

Target Alert and Heat

AlertLevelEach turn, during the Defenders step
0–2NormalNothing happens.
3–5ElevatedYour Laptop operations get −10%.
6–8InvestigatingLaptop operations −10%. Trace: +1 Heat for every full 3 Trail. Hunt: if you have a Foothold and Trail is 2 or more, roll the d10: on 1–3 you lose the Foothold (if you have an Implant, lose the Implant instead). Running operations that need the Foothold are cancelled.
9–11Incident responseThe first time you reach this level: credentials are reset (lose Admin credentials and MFA bypass) and the VPN is patched (Exploit the VPN no longer works). Every turn: Laptop operations −20%, Trace as above, and Hunt on 1–5.
12LockdownContract failed.

Heat never drops during a contract. At 10, police raid you and the contract fails, unless you hired the Lawyer. Lying low (a turn with no Noise) lowers Target Alert by 2, but while Alert is 6 or more, Trace and Hunt still happen every turn.

Optional variant: the rival

Once the solo game makes sense, add a rival racing for the same ledger:

  • Track the rival on a separate line: Foothold → Admin credentials → Route → MFA bypass → Exfiltrated.
  • At the end of turns 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15 (Day 1 Evening, Day 2 Dawn, Day 2 Evening, Day 3 Dawn, Day 3 Evening), the rival advances one step and adds +1 Target Alert.
  • If the rival reaches "Exfiltrated" first, you lose.
  • Close the door: after you gain Admin credentials, use a Laptop slot for 4 hours (Noise 1) to delay the rival's next advance by 3 turns. Usable once.

Questions to answer after each session

  1. Did you face real decisions most turns, or were there obvious best moves?
  2. Did you understand what each action does? Did the "what it is" lines help?
  3. Did the parts of the day make sense? Did you know when to do what?
  4. Were there turns where you had nothing useful to start?
  5. Did running operations side by side feel good, or like bookkeeping?
  6. Did Target Alert and Heat change how you played? When did you lie low, and did it help you recover?
  7. Did you use physical operations? Were they worth the travel and the risk?
  8. Did you buy gear ahead of time and plan around it? Did money matter?
  9. Which card did you never play, and why? Which did you always play?
  10. How did the ending feel: a tense finish, or a formality?
  11. How long did a contract take?

Tuning knobs

Change one thing at a time between sessions and note it in the log: success chances (±10%), Noise values, the Alert thresholds, how much Alert drops in a quiet turn (now 2), starting cash, the deadline (16 turns), Hunt odds, and gear prices.
